## Facts

Only what people said and did. Quotes are verbatim. *(obs.)* = observed by the interviewer, not said by the person.

**A**
- Got the laptop around ten on the first day.
- "I needed to change my groupware password and I didn't know where."
- Tried the settings, then the help page. "I basically lost half the day on that."
- Didn't ask anyone at first.
- Kept a list of questions in a notes app; asked the buddy all of them at once on Friday.
- "Some of them took five seconds to answer."
- Showed the list: 14 questions, most of them "where do I find..." *(obs.)*

**B**
- "Everyone told me 'it's all on the wiki'."
- Searched "card" on the wiki and got forty pages.
- Needed to know who to apply to for the corporate card and how to book a meeting room.
- Asked a different person every time — whoever was sitting closest.
- "Sometimes they didn't know either and sent me to someone else."
- Tried the chatbot once, then asked a person anyway.

**C**
- New hires come with questions "three or four times a day."
- "Every time I get interrupted, it takes me about twenty minutes to get back into my work."
- Questions are "mostly the same ones every time. Password, corporate card, meeting rooms, who to ask about leave."
- Got up from the desk 4 times in 3 hours for new-hire questions. *(obs.)*
- 3 of those 4 times, the answer was sending a wiki link. *(obs.)*
- Said "I should write this down somewhere" once. *(obs.)*

## Insights (shared by all three)

**1. The same few topics come up again and again: password, corporate card, meeting rooms.**
- A: "I needed to change my groupware password and I didn't know where."
- B: "The corporate card. Who do I apply to? And how do I book a meeting room?"
- C: "Mostly the same ones every time. Password, corporate card, meeting rooms, who to ask about leave."

**2. A written source exists or was tried, but the question still ends up with a person.**
- A: "I tried the settings, then the help page." … "On Friday I asked my buddy all of them at once."
- B: "I tried the chatbot once. It answered, but I wasn't sure the answer was right, so I asked a person anyway."
- C *(obs.)*: 3 of 4 times, the answer was sending a wiki link.

**Shared by two only:** a stated cost in time. A: "I basically lost half the day on that." C: "It takes me about twenty minutes to get back into my work."

## Problem statement

> New hires in their first week need to know right away what to do today and who to ask when they are stuck,
> because they are not sure they may ask, so they hold their questions and lose half a day, and their buddy gets interrupted three or four times a day by the same questions.

## How might we...

1. How might we help a new hire find the answer to a "where do I find..." question in under a minute, without asking anyone?
2. How might we make it clear to new hires, from day one, which questions they can ask and who to ask them?
3. How might we let a buddy answer a repeated question once, so the next new hire doesn't need to interrupt them for it?
